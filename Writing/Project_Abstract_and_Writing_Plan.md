# Project Abstract and Writing Plan

## Draft abstract (results omitted)

Marine plastic pollution is a persistent coastal-management challenge in South Africa, where rivers, stormwater systems, harbours and urban runoff can transport debris into the sea. Repeatable information on the location and movement of floating material is needed to support prevention, clean-up and field validation. Existing field and optical-satellite observations are valuable, but their spatial and temporal coverage can be limited by cost, cloud cover and daylight.

This study aims to develop a synthetic aperture radar (SAR)-based training dataset and machine-learning approach for detecting floating marine plastics in the South African Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ). SAR offers cloud- and daylight-independent observation, but its use for marine-plastic detection is constrained by the scarcity of annotated, locally relevant SAR data. The study therefore addresses both the data and modelling gaps: it constructs a South African dataset that relates satellite observations of marine debris to SAR scenes and environmental context, rather than treating plastic as having one fixed radar signature.

The research covers the coastal environments of Durban, Gqeberha and Cape Town. These locations represent contrasting urban, riverine and port-related pathways for marine plastic, allowing the approach to be assessed under varied coastal conditions. Sentinel-1 SAR is the principal Earth-observation input and is supported by optical satellite imagery, existing labelled marine-debris datasets, observation records, and biophysical variables including wind and ocean currents. Existing datasets provide reference examples for model development, while matched observations and satellite scenes in the South African study areas are used to create locally relevant labels. When debris observations and SAR acquisitions are separated in time, the likely movement of floating material and the resulting uncertainty are considered during label alignment. The prepared data will be used to extract labelled image samples and to examine the contribution of radar, optical and environmental information to distinguishing debris from other marine and coastal features.

The primary model will be an Attention U-Net, an attention-guided encoder-decoder architecture for spatial detection. It will first undergo self-supervised learning on unlabelled SAR imagery so that it can learn representations from the much larger pool of available data, before supervised fine-tuning on the assembled labelled dataset. Model performance will be assessed across the three study areas and under differing environmental and acquisition conditions, including potential confusion with other marine features and uncertainty in the reference labels. The expected contribution is a reproducible South African framework for creating SAR-ready marine-plastic training data and evaluating Attention U-Net-based detection models. It will provide an evidence base for further research, targeted field validation, and more informed marine-plastic prevention, clean-up and coastal-management strategies.

## Writing plan

**Target:** 400-450 words (approximately one page in 11-12 pt font with standard margins); one paragraph per component below; no citations unless the submission instructions require them.

1. **Open with the societal and South African problem (50-60 words).** Briefly connect marine plastic to South African coastal pathways such as rivers, stormwater and harbours. State why repeatable location information matters for prevention, clean-up and field validation, then introduce the monitoring constraint. Do not list all consequences of marine plastic; the abstract should move quickly to the research.

2. **State the aim and research gap (75-85 words).** Give the project aim almost exactly: to develop a SAR-based training dataset and machine-learning approach for detecting floating marine plastics in the South African EEZ. Explain that SAR is cloud- and daylight-independent, but that locally relevant annotated SAR data are scarce. Make clear that the research addresses both the dataset and modelling gaps.

3. **Locate the study and summarise the evidence base (105-115 words).** Name Durban, Gqeberha and Cape Town and explain why they offer contrasting urban, riverine and port-related coastal settings. Identify Sentinel-1 as the core sensor, with optical imagery, existing labelled datasets, observation records, wind and ocean-current information as supporting evidence. Explain the distinction between reference examples from existing datasets and locally relevant labels derived from matched South African observations and scenes.

4. **Describe the dataset and model design (110-120 words).** Explain that the project prepares SAR and supporting data, creates labelled image samples, and considers drift and uncertainty when observations and imagery are separated in time. State that it examines the value of radar, optical and environmental information for distinguishing debris from other features. Name Attention U-Net as the primary attention-guided encoder-decoder model. State that self-supervised learning pre-trains it on unlabelled SAR imagery before supervised fine-tuning on the assembled labels. Avoid specific processing branches, SSL objectives, loss functions, software and data-pipeline terminology.

5. **Explain evaluation and limitations (55-65 words).** State that performance will be assessed across locations and environmental/acquisition conditions. Include uncertainty in the reference labels and potential confusion with other marine features. This makes the study credible without reporting any results.

6. **Finish with contribution and value (45-55 words).** Promise a reproducible South African dataset and framework for evaluating Attention U-Net-based SAR detection, not a completed operational system. Link the contribution to future research, targeted field validation, prevention and clean-up planning, and coastal management.

7. **Final edit pass (10 minutes).** Check that every sentence answers one of five questions: why it matters, what is unknown, what data are used, what will be done, or what the project contributes. Remove result language (for example, "demonstrates," "improves," or performance values) until results are available. Confirm that "SAR" is defined at first use and that the abstract still makes sense without technical diagrams.
